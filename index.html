On the Sell Side

Both apps want the same seller at the same nervous moment. I walked through each one with the same car on the same day. This is what I saw, where trust breaks down, and what AI could actually fix.

The argument in one sentence

Cars24 isn't losing sellers because its price is wrong. It's losing them because the price feels random, with no explanation behind it. 57% walk away before the inspector ever arrives.

01 — Context

The stakes are higher than they look

India's used car market crossed five million transactions in FY25, growing faster than new car sales for the third year in a row. Two digital platforms have scaled inside that market: Cars24, which started as India's first online dealer-auction in 2015, and Spinny, which came in a few years later with a buy-refurbish-resell model. Both want to be the platform sellers trust when they decide to let go of their car.

The Sell journey is where trust is earned or lost in minutes. The seller arrives nervous. They don't really know what their car is worth, they assume the dealer will lowball them, and someone they know has probably told them a horror story. The platform that takes the most uncertainty out wins. The one that adds new uncertainty loses the booking, the deal, or the customer for good.

What follows is a first-hand walkthrough of both apps on the same day, with the same car. I'm looking at where each one loses sellers, and what AI could do about it. The question isn't who has the better app. It's who's losing sellers they shouldn't be.

02 — Business Models

Two different bets on the same market

The financials tell the story first. Cars24 closed FY25 at ₹6,233 crore, down 10% year on year, with losses up slightly to ₹543 crore.[1] Spinny posted ₹4,657 crore, up 25%, with losses down 28% to ₹423 crore.[2] Cars24 is larger but shrinking. Spinny is smaller but accelerating. The two trajectories trace back to bets each made years ago about what kind of company they wanted to be.

Cars24 runs an asset-light auction. It doesn't actually buy the cars. The seller gets an indicative price from Profecto, an XGBoost model trained on roughly a million inspections, nine million auction events, and 750,000 transacted cars.[3] After the physical inspection, a 30-minute live auction opens up to 20,000+ verified dealers. The highest bid is the final offer, valid for 24 hours. Cars24 makes money on the spread, on financing through its Loans24 NBFC, and on add-ons like Seller Kavach and the Pan-India Pro listing.

Spinny does the opposite. Through its SellRight program, it buys the car itself. Its Star Engine algorithm sets a price, and that price is contractually guaranteed to land within ±5% of the online estimate after the physical inspection.[8] Spinny then refurbishes the car and sells it direct to a buyer, keeping the retail margin. Cars that don't meet criteria are turned down or pushed to its Truebil subsidiary.

Here's what that means for the seller. Cars24's Profecto sets the online estimate, but doesn't set the final price. The dealers do. Spinny's Star Engine sets both. That one difference is behind almost every friction point below.

04 — Friction Analysis

Five places where sellers disappear

The sell funnel has four points where people drop off: after the online estimate, after the booking, after the inspection itself, and after the offer. Each one is real revenue walking out. Below is where the loss is actually happening, and why.

01

The price gap that nobody explains

First-hand Same 2024 Tata Nexon, same day. Cars24 quoted ₹8.77L–₹9.53L[c5]. Spinny quoted ₹6.50L–₹8.04L[s11]. The gap at the midpoint is around ₹1.5 to ₹2 lakh. Neither app explains how it got there.

This matters for two reasons. First, sellers routinely check both apps in one sitting. They see two very different numbers, and they have no reason to trust either. Second, Cars24 says Profecto hits within ±5% of the final price half the time, and within ±10% in 80% of cases.[3] Sounds fine until you do the math. Half of all cars are off by more than 5%. On a ₹9 lakh car, that's a ₹45,000+ swing on the optimistic side. And that swing is exactly the surprise sellers get at the inspection. The model knows which features cause the variance. Putting that reasoning in front of the seller is solvable. Neither company has done it.

The gap between the two estimates also has a real explanation. Spinny's Star Engine predicts what Spinny itself will pay, after factoring in retail margin. Profecto predicts what a dealer will bid in an open auction. A popular variant in a hot market does better on Cars24. A weak car in a soft market does better on Spinny's guarantee. The seller can't know any of this if no one tells them.

02

Sixty percent never book an inspection

A published Cars24 product case study reported that on direct and brand channels, only 43% of users who hit the price estimate go on to book an inspection.[6] The other 57% leave. The study blamed two things: the gap between the quote and what sellers expect at inspection, and the lack of any explanation behind the number.

This is a trust problem, not a UX problem. The book button is right there. The booking is free. The inspector even comes to you. And still, more than half the people who entered their plate and saw a price never book. They aren't confused by the interface. They just don't believe the number.

Spinny's intent question[s9] is the only place either app admits sellers aren't all the same. Someone who says "just checking" should see a different experience than someone who says "immediately." Cars24 treats them the same, loses the browser, and never follows up. A 30-day nurture flow could win some of them back later.

03

The inspection anxiety window

Cars24 tells the seller their car expert will be assigned 30 minutes before the slot starts.[c8] It's meant to look efficient. In practice, it creates a 30-minute window of pure anxiety. The seller is home, the car is washed and waiting, they don't know who's coming, and they can't track or confirm anything. Forum threads have plenty of stories of inspectors showing up late, cancelling without warning, or just not showing up.[9]

JM Financial pegged the inspection-to-deal conversion at OLX Autos at around 10% before CarTrade shut it down in late 2023.[7] Cars24 and Spinny do better than that, but the pattern of price disappointment at inspection says the drop-off is still meaningful.

Spinny handles this better in two ways. The Spinny Hub option[s12] lets sellers drive in for a more thorough inspection in a controlled space. And Spinny actually tells the seller it'll take 45–60 minutes inside a 90-minute slot. That's clearer than Cars24's hour blocks.

04

Cancellation treated as exit, not signal

When a seller cancels on Cars24, they get four options[c10]: "I don't want to sell my car right now," "I've sold my car elsewhere," "I'm not available at the scheduled time," and "My reason is not listed." Cars24 stops there. No follow-up, no help offered, no attempt to find out what actually went wrong.

Spinny shows eight[s19], including "Getting better price somewhere else," "Offered price is not attractive," and "Inspector asked to cancel." Those last two name failures that are Spinny's fault, not the seller's. And before the cancellation goes through, Spinny pops a modal: "Need help with Evaluation? Get a Call Back."[s18] The seller who was about to leave gets offered a real conversation instead of a confirmation screen.

Spinny treats cancellation as the start of a retention conversation. Cars24 treats it as a form submission. Eight honest reasons give you something to work with. Four polite ones give you noise.

"Inspector asked to cancel" and "Offered price is not attractive" are reasons Spinny lets sellers pick. Letting them name the failure is honest, and useful. You can't fix what you won't name.
05

The RC transfer black hole

Both apps promise free RC (Registration Certificate) transfer after the sale. Both routinely take 60 to 180 days. The delays have real costs. Sellers have racked up challans in their name from the new owner's driving. One Spinny seller had their license impounded eight months later, after ₹50,000+ in fines piled up. A Bengaluru woman was summoned for a hit-and-run she had nothing to do with.[9]

Cars24's Orbit product, plus its ₹118 crore acquisition of CarInfo in January 2026, looks like a real attempt to fix this at the data layer. CarInfo has 12 million MAUs and live lookups for registration, insurance, PUC, FASTag, and challan status.[5] But on both platforms, RC status communication to the seller is still a black box. They get the money on day one and then have no real way to know whether they're still legally exposed months later.

05 — AI Strategy

Where AI closes the gap

Cars24 has by far the most publicly disclosed AI stack in Indian used cars: Profecto for price prediction, Profundus for computer vision at inspection, Autonaut as a RAG-based consumer chatbot, a self-hosted PaddleOCR document pipeline, and an ElevenLabs voice-agent setup that handles roughly 3 million outbound call minutes a month, with +35% conversion and −50% calling cost.[4] Spinny has barely talked about its AI publicly.

The gap nobody has closed is pre-inspection. Sellers don't know what to expect. The inspector arrives. The offer is a surprise. That's where 57–60% of the funnel falls out. Every intervention below targets that one problem, and every one of them maps to a system already running in production.

Before the systems, here's what each one actually does for the seller. The point isn't cleverness. It's clarity.

01 Addresses Friction 1 + 2
Pre-inspection condition elicitor agent
A conversational agent that runs between the online estimate and the booking. It asks about the car's actual condition: dents, service gaps, past accidents. Each answer updates the price range live, with a one-line explanation. "A front bumper dent typically takes ₹8,000–₹12,000 off at inspection." The seller shows up with a tighter, more honest number. The inspector shows up with a baseline already declared. Price-shock cancellations drop. Build this as a LangGraph multi-step agent: one node per question category, memory carried across the session, and a final price-adjustment summary as output. Profecto already produces the SHAP values that explain each feature's weight. The missing piece is just putting them in natural language.
I have built this
The JindalX real-time claim assistant is the same shape: a LangGraph agent that gathers structured inputs in a live session, feeds them into a downstream decision model, and returns a natural-language decision. It's deployed across 200+ concurrent revenue-cycle agents. The Cars24 version swaps claim fields for condition signals, and the insurance payor API for Profecto. The orchestration pattern is identical.
02 Addresses Friction 2
Intent-aware journey routing
Spinny's "When are you planning to sell?" is the only intent signal either app collects, and Spinny does nothing with it. Someone who says "just checking" should enter a 30-day nurture flow with weekly market alerts for their specific car. Someone who says "immediately" should get the first available slot and a pre-filled document upload prompt. This doesn't need new infrastructure. It's just branching on top of what Cars24 already has.
I have built this
The 25% accuracy improvement on Clari's sales copilot came from upstream signal classification: categorising prospect intent from sparse input, then routing to the right retrieval and response strategy. Same problem here. One early signal, branching downstream experiences, measurable conversion impact. The model is a single-question intent classifier. The hard part is building out the content of each nurture path, not the routing logic.
03 Addresses Friction 3
Proactive pre-inspection voice agent
Cars24 already runs ElevenLabs voice agents for outbound post-booking calls.[4] Extend the same pipeline to a pre-inspection call sent 60 minutes before the slot, with the inspector's name, ETA, and a quick checklist of what to have ready. The 30-minute anxiety window goes away, and operations don't change. During the inspection itself, a WhatsApp update ("Exterior done, moving to engine bay") keeps the seller anchored. This is an orchestration change to an existing system, not a new one.
I have built this
The SisterAgency auto pipeline chains two agents end-to-end with WebSocket-based real-time messaging at each step. The pattern is the same one needed here: trigger on event, run agent, push status to user in real time, hand off on completion. The Cars24 version triggers on appointment confirmation, runs the voice agent, and sends WhatsApp updates through the inspection lifecycle.
04 Addresses Friction 4
Cancellation winback agent
Spinny's "Get a Call Back" modal is the right instinct, just done manually. An AI version reads the cancellation reason, looks up the specific car and quote, and opens with a real counter. "You said the price wasn't attractive. Given your Dark Edition trim and current Noida dealer demand, ₹9.1L is achievable at auction. Want to reschedule?" That isn't a generic save attempt. It's a data-driven response to a stated objection. Cars24 has the auction-demand signal already. The agent reads the cancellation event, pulls the relevant pricing context, sends a targeted response, and escalates to a human if the conversation goes past two exchanges.
I have built this
The JindalX claim assistant is triggered automatically on claim creation and responds in real time within the same session. No queued follow-up, no batch processing. The winback agent is the same pattern: event-triggered, context-retrieved, session-bound, with a human escalation path. The only difference is the trigger (cancellation instead of claim creation) and the retrieval source (Cars24 auction demand instead of payor eligibility data).
05 Addresses Friction 5
RC transfer status intelligence layer
The CarInfo acquisition gives Cars24 live access to VAHAN, hypothecation NOC status, challan lookups, and Form-35 filing status across all RTOs.[5] The missing piece is a seller-facing layer that pushes this data out. A weekly WhatsApp like "RC transfer is pending at Lucknow RTO. Current processing time there is 45 days, and you're on day 22" isn't technically hard. It needs CarInfo data, a notification pipeline, and a status template. The seller goes from assuming the worst to actually knowing where they stand.
I have built this
The Azure OpenAI eligibility pipeline at JindalX processes 1,000-plus records a day at 99%+ accuracy: structured data from payor APIs goes in, natural language eligibility status comes out, delivered with no human in the loop per record. Cutting manual effort by 60% came from applying the same transformation to every record at scale. The RC status layer is the same architecture: structured data in (CarInfo VAHAN API), natural language status out, delivered weekly to every post-sale seller.
06 — The Reimagined Sell Flow

What this looks like for the seller

The five interventions aren't abstract. Stitched together, they make one continuous flow where the seller never loses footing. Here's what a single sell session feels like once Cars24 ships them.

Lands on the Sell page
Before entering anything, sees a live local band: "2024 Tata Nexons in Kanpur are selling for ₹8.5L–₹9.5L this week." The number stops feeling invented.
Enters plate
Quote shows ₹9.0L expected · confidence: high · range: ₹8.7L–₹9.3L. Below it, one tappable line: "Why this price?"
Taps "Why this price?"
Three drivers in plain language: trim, RTO-level demand, mileage band. Each with a number attached. Then: "Want to sharpen this?"
Answers the condition agent
Each answer adjusts the range live. "Front bumper dent → −₹10K. Service overdue → −₹6K. Final expected: ₹8.84L." They are no longer guessing what the inspector will say.
Books inspection
An hour before the slot, a voice agent calls: "Rohan is your evaluator, ETA 2:15 PM. Keep RC and one ID handy." The 30-minute anxiety window is gone.
Inspection ends
Final offer: ₹8.92L, inside the predicted band. The auction runs. The price holds. They sell.

The same seller in today's flow sees ₹8.77L–₹9.53L, walks into the inspection blind, gets an offer of ₹7.4L with no explanation, and leaves. The car, the dealers, and the underlying price haven't changed. The only thing the new flow rebuilds is the trust around them.

Phase 1 — Ship this first
Explainable pricing with confidence and range

Of the five, one earns the right to ship first: surface Profecto's existing SHAP values as a confidence range and a "why this price" tap. The capability is already there. The model produces these explanations and then drops them at the API boundary. There's no new agent, no new pipeline, no operational change. And it directly addresses the single biggest drop-off in the funnel.

If even part of the 57% drop-off is recovered, this is the highest-leverage feature Cars24 can ship in FY27. It's also the foundation the other four interventions build on.
07 — Conclusion

The real competition is for seller trust, not seller volume

Cars24 and Spinny aren't the same company with different logos. They've made very different bets on the same moment: when someone decides to sell a car they've owned for years, and just hopes the platform won't screw them over.

Cars24 wins on speed, reach, and the depth of its AI stack. Spinny wins on price certainty, a clean post-booking experience, and a cancellation flow that asks better questions. Both lose sellers at the same point: between the online estimate and the inspection. That 57–60% drop-off isn't a UX problem. It's a trust problem. And in 2026, trust problems are AI problems, because the tech to explain a price, personalise a journey, predict a cancellation, and automate a follow-up isn't research anymore. It's production.

None of the five interventions above is speculative. Each one maps to a system already running in production: the JindalX claim assistant, the Azure OpenAI eligibility pipeline, the Clari retrieval and routing work, the SisterAgency real-time pipeline. The engineering patterns are well known. The real question is whether the product team is aimed at the right problem.

The right problem isn't getting more people to enter their plate number. It's making the people who already did trust the platform enough to be home when the inspector rings the bell.

Cars24 already has the best pricing engine in Indian used cars. The opportunity isn't to make it more accurate. It's to make sellers trust it.
